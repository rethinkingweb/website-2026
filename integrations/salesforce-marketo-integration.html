The Basics

What Does This Integration Do?

Marketo and Salesforce ship with a native sync, and for basic lead and contact mirroring it works. What it doesn't solve is the argument: marketing says it delivered qualified leads, sales says they weren't qualified, and nobody can point at revenue to settle it.

This engagement tunes the sync properly — field mappings, lead lifecycle stages, and routing rules — then builds the layer the native connector doesn't: engagement-driven scoring that sales agrees with, campaign membership that survives lead conversion, and closed-loop attribution from first touch through to closed-won revenue.

Methods

How We Build It

The native connector does the heavy lifting on standard objects. Everything beyond that comes from the two REST APIs and careful lifecycle design.

ApproachWhat it isBest forWatch out for
Marketo–Salesforce native syncMarketo's built-in bi-directional connectorLead, contact, account, and opportunity mirroringSync interval isn't instant; field mapping is fiddly to change later
Marketo REST APIREST access to leads, programs, activities, and custom objectsCustom flows the native sync doesn't coverDaily API call quota is easy to exhaust on large databases
Salesforce REST & Bulk APIRecord-level and batch access to Salesforce objectsPushing attribution data back, syncing custom objectsDaily API call limits by edition
Custom middlewarePurpose-built orchestration over both APIsAttribution models, scoring logic, and multi-system flowsNeeds a partner who can maintain it (that's us)

The truth most vendors won't tell you: if the native sync is misconfigured, no amount of extra tooling fixes it — you just automate the mess faster. We audit and correct the native mapping first, and only build custom logic on top once the foundation is right.
