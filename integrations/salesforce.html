The Basics

What Is Salesforce Integration?

Salesforce integration is the process of exchanging data automatically between Salesforce and the other applications your business runs on, so records like accounts, contacts, opportunities, orders, and invoices stay consistent everywhere without manual re-entry. It's how a closed-won opportunity becomes a NetSuite sales order, how a QuickBooks payment lands back on the Salesforce account, and how a Shopify customer appears in your CRM the moment they buy.

Most Salesforce customers run an ERP, an accounting package, an ecommerce store, and a marketing platform alongside the CRM. Every one of those is a potential silo. Integration turns that stack into a single operating picture: sales sees fulfillment and payment status, finance sees the pipeline, and nobody re-keys the same customer into four systems. Done well, it removes hours of manual work each week and eliminates the copy-paste errors that quietly corrupt your reporting.

Methods

How Do You Integrate Salesforce with Other Systems?

Salesforce exposes one of the richest API surfaces of any platform: the REST API for real-time record operations, the SOAP API for contract-based enterprise tooling, Bulk API 2.0 for millions of records asynchronously, Platform Events and the Streaming API for real-time change notifications, and Apex for server-side logic and callouts. On top of these sit MuleSoft and other iPaaS connectors that wrap the APIs in pre-built templates. The right choice depends on your edition, data volumes, API limits, and how standard your requirement is.

ApproachWhat it isBest forWatch out for
REST APIJSON API for real-time, record-level create/read/updateWeb integrations, single-record sync, modern custom buildsDaily API call limits by edition; design for efficiency
Bulk API 2.0Asynchronous batch loading and extraction of large data setsMigrations, nightly syncs, millions of recordsNot real-time; jobs are queued and processed asynchronously
Platform EventsEvent-driven pub/sub over the Streaming APIReal-time triggers, event-driven architectures, low-latency syncEvent allocations and delivery limits; needs a subscriber
MuleSoft / iPaaSPre-built connectors on MuleSoft, Boomi, Celigo, or WorkatoStandard flows (ERP, accounting, ecommerce) going live fastLicence cost forever; MuleSoft especially is enterprise-priced
Custom middlewarePurpose-built orchestration (Make, n8n, or code) using the APIs aboveNon-standard requirements, multi-system workflows, AI automationNeeds a partner who can maintain it (that's us)

The truth most vendors won't tell you: a pre-built connector is genuinely the right answer for many standard requirements. We'll tell you when it is, help you choose and configure it — even if that means MuleSoft or Celigo — and only build custom middleware when your requirement demands it.

Pricing

How Much Does Salesforce Integration Cost?

A typical Salesforce integration costs between $3,000 and $30,000 to implement, depending on the approach. Templated iPaaS connectors sit at the low end plus a monthly platform subscription; fully custom API builds sit at the high end with no recurring platform fee.

ApproachImplementationOngoing
Templated iPaaS connector (Celigo, Workato)$3,000 – $9,000$100 – $800/month platform subscription
iPaaS orchestration (Make, n8n)$5,000 – $15,000$50 – $400/month platform + support retainer
Custom REST / Bulk / Platform Events build$10,000 – $30,000+Support retainer only, no platform fee

Three factors move the number most: how many objects you sync (accounts, contacts, opportunities, orders, invoices), whether flows are one-way or bi-directional, and how much transformation and dedupe logic sits between the systems. Salesforce API limits also matter — a high-volume sync may need Bulk API 2.0 rather than per-record REST calls. We quote fixed prices after a scoping call, so you know the cost before we write a line of code.
