The Basics

What Does This Integration Do?

In most field service businesses the accounting lag is the paperwork lag. A job finishes, the sheet sits in a van or an inbox, and days later someone keys it into QuickBooks — sometimes with the wrong customer, sometimes not at all.

This integration posts completed Zuper jobs into QuickBooks as invoices with the correct customer, line items, and tax, keeps the customer list aligned across both systems, and brings payment status back into Zuper so the field and finance teams see the same reality.

Methods

How We Build It

QuickBooks Online exposes a REST API; QuickBooks Desktop connects through the Web Connector or a sync bridge. Zuper ships a native QuickBooks connector alongside its own REST API. The right method depends on your edition and invoicing rules.

ApproachWhat it isBest forWatch out for
Zuper native QuickBooks connectorPre-built marketplace integration inside ZuperStandard job-to-invoice and payment-back flowsCovers supported fields; edition-dependent
QuickBooks Online APIREST access to invoices, customers, itemsCloud QBO, real-time invoicingGovernance and rate limits on large syncs
QuickBooks Desktop (Web Connector)Scheduled sync bridge to on-prem QuickBooksBusinesses still on DesktopRuns on a schedule, not truly real-time
Custom middleware (n8n / code)Orchestration wrapping both APIsNon-standard tax, multi-entity, job costingNeeds a partner who can maintain it (that's us)

Where a template usually falls short: posting an invoice is the easy half. Matching items to the right service, applying the correct tax code per jurisdiction, and mapping job costing back for profitability is where the accounting detail lives — and where templates stop.
